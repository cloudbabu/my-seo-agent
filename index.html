05 / The bar

We are not buying praise. We are asking for detail.

Criticism is paid at the same rate as a compliment. What does not get paid is anything vague enough that it could have been written about any provider, by anyone, without logging in.

Not this

"AceCloud is great, the GPUs work well and the support team is helpful. Highly recommend to anyone."

True, possibly. But it tells the next engineer nothing, so it does not qualify, however glowing it is.

This

"We moved inference off a multi-GPU box onto a single RTX Pro 6000 instance. Monthly GPU spend dropped roughly 40%, and p95 latency held at 180ms."

A decision, a change, a number. Someone choosing a provider next week can use this.

Both examples above are illustrative, written to show the difference in specificity.

Real
Something you did, not something you could imagine doing.
Specific
A number, a config, a trade-off you weighed.
First-hand
Your workload, your account, your words.
Useful
It teaches, shows, or compares something for the next person.
06 / Story starters

Stuck on the first line? Take one of these.

Pick whichever is true for you, copy the opener, and keep going in your own words. It works for a video script as well as a blog post.

What did you actually build?

Walk through the deploy. A GPU endpoint, a Kubernetes cluster, an object storage setup, and what it took to get it running.

Last month I deployed [workload] on AceCloud. Here is how it went.
What changed in the numbers?

Cost per month, training time, inference latency, provisioning wait. Before and after beats adjectives.

Our [metric] went from [X] to [Y] after we moved to AceCloud.
Why did you pick this over the alternative?

GPU availability, rupee billing, data residency, egress charges, support response. Name the thing that actually tipped it.

We compared AceCloud against [provider]. What decided it was [reason].
What broke, and how did you fix it?

A real troubleshooting story is worth more to the next engineer than a smooth one. Include the rough edges.

We hit [problem] on day two. Here is what we tried and what worked.
What surprised you?

Something easier than you expected, or a limitation you had to design around. Both are useful.

I expected [assumption]. What actually happened was [reality].
What would you tell a friend deciding?

The honest comparison. Where it won, where it did not, and who it suits.

If you are choosing between AceCloud and [provider], here is the honest version.

08 / The fine print, up front

Disclose it, keep it, and we will ask before we use it.

Say that you were rewarded

Most review platforms require a disclosure when there is an incentive involved, and it is the right thing to do regardless. It does not weaken your review, and it protects you if anyone asks later.

A line like this is enough:

Disclosure: I received a reward from AceCloud for taking the time to write this. The experience and the opinions are my own.

The Content Guide covers the exact wording each platform expects, including the ones that handle disclosure through their own form.

Your content stays yours

You own what you publish. It lives on your account, under your name, and you can edit or remove it whenever you like.

If we want to quote you on our site, in a case study, or in a campaign, we will ask you first and show you the context. Nothing gets lifted quietly.

We do not ask for edits to your published content, and we do not withdraw a reward because a review was critical. If a platform removes your post for its own reasons, tell us and we will sort it out case by case.

10 / Before you start

Questions people actually ask.

Do I have to still be an AceCloud customer?
No. Former customers are welcome, and a review from someone who moved on is often more useful than one from someone who stayed. Write from your actual experience, including how it ended.
What if my experience was not all positive?
Then say so. Constructive and specific is exactly what we are after, and a critical review is paid at the same rate as a positive one. What does not get paid is anything fabricated, in either direction.
Do I need to disclose that I got a reward?
On most platforms, yes, and we would recommend it everywhere. Some review sites handle it through their own form. The Content Guide lists what each platform expects, and there is a sample line in section 08 above.
Can I contribute on more than one platform?
Up to three contributions in total, with one review per review platform. Each has to be genuinely different work. The same text pasted across four sites counts once, if at all.
Does my video need editing or good production?
No. A clean screen recording with clear audio is all it takes. We are not judging production quality, and an unpolished walkthrough of something real is worth more than a slick video with nothing in it.
Can I use AI to write it?
You can use it to tidy up your own draft. What you cannot do is hand over a piece you did not write about an experience you did not have. Generated submissions read exactly like generated submissions, and they are not eligible.
How and when do I get paid?
After a person reviews and approves your submission, typically within 5 to 6 business days of you sending the link. We confirm the payout method with you directly at that point.
What if the platform takes my review down later?
Let us know at reviews@acecloud.ai. It is handled case by case under the program terms, and an approved payout is not clawed back because a third-party platform changed its mind.
Is there a deadline?
The program runs through 30 September 2026. Submissions sent on or before that date are reviewed even if the review itself lands afterwards.
